## 1. Purpose

The MASS ecosystem has reached a stage where continued manufacturing can proceed, but potentially protectable intellectual property should no longer depend on memory, scattered conversations, or undocumented repository history.

This doctrine creates a durable system for:

- identifying potentially valuable intellectual property;
- preserving conception, authorship, inventorship, implementation, and disclosure evidence;
- distinguishing patent candidates from trade secrets, copyright assets, trademarks, and ordinary product features;
- preventing accidental public disclosure from silently deciding patent strategy;
- keeping ownership and third-party rights visible;
- preparing a high-quality evidence package for future intellectual-property counsel;
- protecting production from premature legal-process overhead.

This doctrine does **not** authorize a patent filing, trademark filing, public claim of exclusivity, licensing transaction, or representation that any listed concept is legally protectable.

---

## 2. Governing Principle

**Preserve now. Classify deliberately. Disclose carefully. File selectively. License from verified rights.**

MASS shall not attempt to patent everything simply because it is valuable. The ecosystem should develop a portfolio in which the appropriate protection follows the nature and commercial value of the asset.

A feature may be commercially important without being patentable. A patentable feature may be strategically better retained as a trade secret. A product may be strongly protected through trademark, copyright, confidentiality, contracts, execution speed, data, customer relationships, and technical integration without a patent.

## 3. Protection Classes

### 3.1 Patent Candidate

Use for a potentially new and useful technical process, machine, system, method, architecture, or technical improvement that may warrant prior-art and patentability review.

Internal classification as `PATENT-CANDIDATE` means only: **preserve and send for professional evaluation before avoidable public technical disclosure.**

### 3.2 Trade Secret Candidate

Use for valuable non-public information whose value depends materially on secrecy, including proprietary orchestration logic, internal scoring/selection rules, implementation heuristics, operational playbooks, prompt/intelligence configurations, manufacturing methods, or other confidential know-how.

Trade-secret classification requires actual secrecy discipline. Information broadly published or unnecessarily distributed should not be treated as secret merely by label.

### 3.3 Copyright Asset

Use for original expression fixed in a tangible medium, including source code, original documentation, UI artwork, graphics, diagrams, audiovisual work, original written content, and qualifying compilations/arrangements.

Copyright classification does not imply ownership of underlying ideas, functionality, methods, facts, or third-party material incorporated into the work.

### 3.4 Trademark / Brand Asset

Use for names, marks, logos, slogans, product identities, suite identities, and source-identifying presentation that may warrant clearance and registration strategy.

No repository declaration establishes trademark availability. Core names must be professionally cleared before substantial commercial reliance or filing.

### 3.5 Contract / License Controlled Asset

Use where protection and commercialization depend significantly on contractual rights: SaaS access, API use, customer terms, partner/white-label rights, data use, media permissions, contributor rights, enterprise deployment, or authorized derivative use.

### 3.6 No Special Filing / Ordinary Feature

Use deliberately where an asset is useful but does not justify special registration or secrecy beyond normal software ownership and contracts.

## 7. Evidence Preservation Standard

For material IP, preserve:

- canonical Git commit SHA and date;
- relevant source/document paths;
- architecture diagrams and specifications;
- issue/work-order/directive history;
- prototype screenshots or recordings when useful;
- test evidence showing implementation;
- release/deployment date;
- authorship/contributor records;
- external contractor or employee assignment agreements where applicable;
- third-party license notices and dependency records;
- public disclosure dates and copies/links;
- first-use evidence for important marks where available.

Do not rewrite history merely to create cleaner IP evidence. Preserve the genuine chronological record.

## 8. Ownership & Contributor Hygiene

Before commercial scale, MMS should be able to answer for each material asset:

1. Who created or conceived it?
2. In what legal capacity did they contribute?
3. Was the contribution assigned to the intended owning entity?
4. Did the contribution include third-party code, media, data, fonts, models, APIs, templates, or other licensed material?
5. What license governs each material dependency?
6. Are there restrictions on commercial use, sublicensing, attribution, modification, redistribution, model training, public display, or derivative works?
7. Is customer/field media authorized for the intended operational or marketing use?

Do not assume payment to a contractor automatically resolves every IP ownership issue. Preserve executed agreements and obtain counsel review where chain of title is uncertain.

AI-assisted development should be documented honestly. Human inventorship/authorship and registrability questions should be evaluated under then-current law and agency guidance at filing time.

## 14. Product Lifecycle IP Gates

### Gate IP-0 — Continuous Preservation (NOW)

Non-blocking. Preserve commits, authorship, invention candidates, disclosure events, third-party rights, and brand evidence while manufacturing continues.

### Gate IP-1 — Pre-Public Technical Disclosure

Triggered when a high-value technical mechanism is about to be disclosed publicly in meaningful detail.

Required outcome: patent/trade-secret/no-filing decision or documented leadership acceptance of disclosure risk.

### Gate IP-2 — Pre-Commercial Brand Commitment

Triggered before major spend, broad launch, reseller activity, or significant customer acquisition under a core product identity.

Required outcome: trademark clearance strategy for foundational marks and filing decision where justified.

### Gate IP-3 — Stable V1 Release

Triggered when a major software product reaches a stable commercial release.

Required outcome: preserve release snapshot, ownership/contributor evidence, third-party inventory, and decide whether copyright registration is commercially justified.

### Gate IP-4 — External Licensing / Enterprise Scale

Triggered before white-label, OEM, material API licensing, reseller arrangements, enterprise licensing, or significant outside investment/diligence.

Required outcome: verified chain of title, licensing framework, IP schedule, material registrations/applications, and counsel-ready diligence package.

## 17. Decision Matrix

| Question | If YES | If NO |
|---|---|---|
| Is the mechanism potentially technically novel and commercially important? | Patent/trade-secret review | Copyright/contract/ordinary feature review |
| Must competitors see how it works to use the product? | Patent may deserve greater consideration | Trade secret may remain viable |
| Does value depend on keeping implementation unknown? | Trade-secret controls | Other protection may fit better |
| Is it primarily source-identifying branding? | Trademark track | Continue classification |
| Is it primarily original code/art/text/UI expression? | Copyright track | Continue classification |
| Does it contain third-party material? | Verify license/ownership before claiming rights | Preserve authorship evidence |
| Is detailed public disclosure imminent? | Trigger IP-1 | Continue preservation |
| Is the name about to receive major commercial investment? | Trigger IP-2 | Monitor |
| Is the release stable and commercially material? | Trigger IP-3 | Preserve normal version history |

## 19. Closing Doctrine

The ecosystem's defensibility should come from a coordinated portfolio, not a single filing:

**Brand identity + copyrighted implementation + selectively patented inventions + protected trade secrets + clean ownership + disciplined licensing + operational execution + accumulated data/experience.**

The purpose of IP governance is not to slow MASS down. It is to ensure that speed does not accidentally give away choices that should have remained available.

**Preserve the option before spending the option.**
